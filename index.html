Professional Self-Assessment

Welcome to my Computer Science Capstone ePortfolio. This portfolio highlights my growth throughout the Computer Science program and showcases my enhanced Inventory Management System.

Completing my Computer Science degree and putting together this ePortfolio has given me an opportunity to reflect on how much I have grown since I started the program. When I first began, I was mostly focused on getting my programs to work and meeting the requirements of each assignment. As I progressed through the program, I began to understand that software development involves much more than writing code that produces the correct result. I became more comfortable thinking about application design, users, data, performance, testing, security, communication, and collaboration. My capstone brought many of these areas together and gave me an opportunity to reflect on how my approach to software development has changed.

One area where I have grown is working and communicating in collaborative environments. Most of my courses included weekly discussion posts and responses to classmates, which gave me opportunities to see how other students approached the same problems. Even when working individually, reading other students' ideas showed me that there can be several ways to solve the same problem. I also practiced explaining my own ideas and responding to different perspectives. Feedback from classmates and instructors helped me look at my work differently and make improvements. These experiences helped me become more comfortable communicating technical ideas and receiving feedback. I recognize that being able to communicate effectively is important in professional software development because developers need to work with teammates, managers, clients, and other stakeholders who may have different levels of technical knowledge.

My understanding of software engineering has also changed throughout the program. Earlier in my studies, I was primarily concerned with getting the correct result and completing the requirements of an assignment. As I gained more experience, I began paying greater attention to how software is organized, how easy it will be to maintain, how users will interact with it, and what could go wrong. Returning to my Inventory Management System for the capstone was especially valuable because I was able to look at my earlier work with a more experienced perspective. Instead of simply accepting the original implementation, I evaluated where the application could be improved and considered how those improvements would affect the overall user experience. This taught me that software engineering is an ongoing process that involves reviewing, maintaining, testing, and improving software over time.

I also developed a better understanding of how data structures and algorithms support practical software development. Earlier in the program, I tended to think of these concepts primarily as topics that needed to be learned for individual assignments. Over time, I began to recognize them as tools for solving problems and improving how an application handles information. Working on my inventory application helped me make that connection because I had to think about how inventory information should be organized, searched, filtered, and presented to users. This experience improved my ability to look beyond whether a feature works and consider whether the approach I use is appropriate for the problem. I now have a better appreciation for the role that algorithms and data structures play in creating applications that are organized, efficient, and useful.

Database development became another important part of my growth. Earlier in the program, I primarily viewed a database as a place to store the information an application needed. Through my coursework and capstone, I developed a better understanding of how database design affects the reliability, security, and maintainability of an entire application. Working with an existing database also taught me to think carefully about how changes can be introduced without disrupting information that is already being used. This experience helped me become more deliberate when making database decisions and showed me that good database development requires planning rather than simply storing data. I also learned that the database and application should be considered together because changes in one can directly affect the other.

Security was one of the areas where I experienced the most growth during the capstone. Earlier in my program, I tended to think about security as something that could be addressed after the main functionality was complete. My capstone changed that perspective. I began thinking about security as something that needs to be considered throughout the development process, including how users authenticate, how sensitive information is protected, how input is validated, and what actions should require additional protection. Testing incorrect logins, invalid input, duplicate accounts, and protected inventory actions also helped me understand the importance of thinking about how an application could be misused rather than only testing the expected user experience. This experience made me more aware of my responsibility as a developer to protect users and their information.

Testing also became more meaningful to me as I worked on the capstone. In earlier courses, I often viewed testing as a way to verify that a program produced the expected result. During the capstone, I began to understand that effective testing also requires considering unexpected situations and incorrect user behavior. Testing different authentication scenarios, invalid information, and protected actions encouraged me to think about what could fail and how the application should respond. This changed the way I approach development because I now see testing as part of the development process rather than simply a final step after the coding is complete.

The Inventory Management System is an important part of my ePortfolio because it represents my growth over time. The original application reflects my skills at an earlier point in the Computer Science program, while the enhanced version demonstrates how those skills developed through additional coursework and experience. Returning to my own older work allowed me to compare the way I approached software development then with the way I approach it now. I was able to identify areas that could be improved, consider the needs of users, and apply concepts from different areas of the program to one project. This experience showed me how the different subjects I studied are connected rather than existing as completely separate areas of computer science.

Building this ePortfolio has also helped me think more seriously about my professional goals. I want to continue improving my programming and software development skills and work on applications that are useful, reliable, and secure. I know there is still a great deal for me to learn, especially because technology continues to change, but I feel more prepared to continue learning and take on new challenges. The Computer Science program has taught me that being a good developer is not only about writing code. It is also about understanding the problem, considering the users, communicating with others, making thoughtful design decisions, protecting information, testing the result, and being willing to improve the software.

Looking back at my Computer Science program, I can see a real difference in how I approach software development compared to when I started. I am more comfortable solving problems, working with databases, applying data structures and algorithms, communicating technical ideas, considering security, and evaluating my own work. I have also learned that professional growth does not end when a program or degree is completed. Technology will continue to change, and I will need to continue learning and adapting throughout my career.

My ePortfolio brings these areas together and gives me an opportunity to demonstrate that growth through work I have actually completed and improved. The capstone was especially valuable because it allowed me to take something I created earlier in my education and approach it with the knowledge and perspective I developed throughout the program. I believe the experience I gained through my coursework, my capstone, and the process of creating this ePortfolio has given me a strong foundation for the next stage of my career in computer science.

Inventory Management System

My Inventory Management System is an Android application originally developed for CS-360. The application was designed to help users manage inventory by allowing them to add, update, delete, search, and organize inventory items.

For my CS-499 capstone, I returned to the original application and enhanced it to demonstrate the knowledge and skills I developed throughout the Computer Science program. The primary enhancements focused on software engineering and design, algorithms and data structures, and database development. I also added additional security, authentication, validation, and testing features.

Software Engineering and Design

The capstone allowed me to improve an existing application rather than simply creating a new project from the beginning. I reviewed the original application, identified areas that could be improved, and implemented new functionality while maintaining the existing features.

I improved the application's organization and user experience by adding authentication, input validation, improved inventory interaction, and protection for sensitive actions. These changes required me to consider how different parts of the application work together and how improvements could be made without disrupting existing functionality.

Algorithms and Data Structures

The application uses an ArrayList to manage inventory items that are displayed through a RecyclerView. I also implemented searching and sorting functionality to help users find and organize inventory information.

Features such as displaying inventory items alphabetically and identifying low-stock and out-of-stock items allowed me to apply data structures and algorithmic concepts to practical problems. Working with these features helped me better understand how data can be organized, searched, filtered, and displayed to improve the efficiency and usability of an application.

Database Development

The application uses SQLite to store inventory information. During the capstone, I expanded the database by adding a users table, database constraints, an index for inventory names, and database versioning.

One important part of this work was learning how to make database changes without losing existing information. This helped me understand how database design affects application functionality, reliability, security, and maintainability.

Other Enhancements Completed

In addition to the primary software engineering, algorithms and data structures, and database improvements, I implemented several additional features to improve the security, reliability, and usability of the application.

  • User Authentication: Added account creation and login functionality so users must authenticate before accessing the inventory.
  • Password Security: Implemented PBKDF2 password hashing with randomly generated salts instead of storing passwords as plain text.
  • Input Validation: Added validation for usernames, passwords, and inventory information to prevent invalid or incomplete data from being submitted.
  • Duplicate Account Prevention: Added database checks to prevent users from creating accounts with usernames that already exist.
  • Inventory Searching: Added functionality that allows users to search for inventory items by name.
  • Inventory Sorting: Added functionality to display inventory items alphabetically.
  • Inventory Filtering: Added functionality to identify low-stock and out-of-stock inventory items.
  • Protected Inventory Actions: Required password verification before allowing the user to clear the entire inventory.
  • Testing: Tested incorrect logins, duplicate accounts, missing fields, short passwords, and incorrect passwords when attempting to clear the inventory.

Artifact Reflection

This artifact demonstrates how I was able to take an existing application and improve it using knowledge gained throughout the Computer Science program. The enhanced version demonstrates my growth in software engineering and design, algorithms and data structures, database development, security, testing, and user-focused application development.

Returning to an application I had created earlier in the program was especially valuable because I was able to recognize problems and improvement opportunities that I probably would not have noticed when I first developed it. The capstone showed me that software development is an ongoing process and that a working application can continue to be improved as my knowledge and experience grow.
